How to read each topic

PYQ → Ghatna Chakra → recall → PYQ

You never “finish a chapter”. You finish a topic when you can answer every PYQ on it and the likely next variant.

  1. Open the PYQs for the topic first (15–20 min). Solve them cold. Write a one-line “question map”: what was asked, and in which format (pair, match, order, statement).
  2. Read only the matching Ghatna Chakra portion, plus the rest of the same table or list. If a PYQ asked three Ramsar sites of UP, learn all of them; if it asked one Peshwa pair, learn the whole Peshwa order.
  3. Read for the format UPPSC uses. For every fact ask: could it be paired (X→place), ordered (earliest→latest, west→east), made half-false in a statement, or turned into an Assertion–Reason? Note the item that makes each work.
  4. Active recall: close the book and rebuild the list or table from memory. Only then check.
  5. Re-solve the same PYQs and log every miss in the error log with its cause.

In 2025 only one of the 129 static questions (everything except Current Affairs) was a plain direct question — the rest were statement, code, match, pair, sequence or A–R. Learning a fact without its neighbours in the list will not be enough.

Go beyond Ghatna Chakra only when a PYQ fact is missing from it (usually UP-specific items or the newest data). Use the PYQ itself as the list, or one standard NCERT chapter if a concept (monsoon mechanism, ecological succession) is genuinely unclear. Do not restart full UPSC books.

Daily & weekly loop

Flexible blocks, not a timetable

Each study-day (4–5 hours)

  • Core, about 2¾ hours: the current subject, PYQ-first as above.
  • Recall, 45 minutes: rebuild today's lists from memory; add to the note sheets.
  • PYQ revisit, 30 minutes: error-log items due today (re-solve after 1, 3 and 7 days) plus 10 PYQs from an already-finished subject.
  • Spaced sheets, 30 minutes: one note sheet from an earlier subject, in rotation.
  • From study-day 26: the last block becomes a 45-minute Current Affairs slot.

Every sixth study-day: consolidation

  • No new topics.
  • One mixed set of 50 PYQs from finished subjects, timed at 60 minutes.
  • Clear the error log: every item re-solved or turned into a one-liner.
  • Revise the week's note sheets once, from memory first.
  • Tick the “completed when…” items you can honestly tick; if behind by more than two days, drop P3 items ahead.

Error log columns

Question (year, number) · subject and topic · cause: fact gap half-true statement wrong order pair mix-up missed NOT · correct fact · re-solve dates.

1. Indian Polity

168 PYQs · 14.0% · ≈21 per paper

Target: 7 study-daysPosition: static pass, #1
0 / 0 ticked

Compact syllabus, two topics asked in all eight papers (Schedules/Parts/Articles and Panchayati Raj), and the questions are fact-exact, which suits Ghatna Chakra. Highest return per study-day, so it goes first.

78 of 168 were direct, but 2025 had none: 5 two-statement, 5 multiple-select, 5 match, 4 A–R, 3 sequence. Read every fact as a statement you might have to judge true or false.

P1Study thoroughly

P2Solid but selective

P3Skim only

Extract while reading

  • Article number → heading, for about 60 articles (UPPSC matches articles to bodies and subjects)
  • Part number → subject, and all 12 Schedules
  • Every year: amendments, committees, Acts
  • Procedure lines: who appoints, who removes, to whom the resignation goes, who presides
  • Numbers: members, tenure, age, seats (UP's 80 and 31), days of absence
  • Firsts: first woman Speaker, first CEC, first Municipal Corporation
  • Not in GC: UP-specific polity (UPPSC, UP seats, UP Day) — take from the PYQs themselves

Target: 9 study-daysPosition: static pass, #2

Largest static block: about 28 questions a paper, and three topics appear in every paper. Map-driven facts that stick once drilled, so it follows Polity while your reading rhythm is fresh.

Match (34) and pair (31) questions are common and 2025 had no direct ones. Most questions are X→location or order-by-direction, so the atlas matters as much as the text.

P1Study thoroughly

P2Solid but selective

P3Skim only

Extract while reading

  • For each feature: country/state, and its position (west→east, north→south, largest→smallest)
  • For each river: origin, mouth, left/right tributaries, dams, cities on it
  • For each mineral: top 2–3 states and the named mines
  • Local winds and cyclones by country
  • Census 2011: top 3 and bottom 3 in each ranking

What to skip or skim

Low yield in the 2018–25 papers

Skim once, no notes

  • Public finance and taxation theory — 1 question in 8 years
  • Planning history and Five-Year Plan details — none since 2020
  • Banking and monetary mechanics beyond WPI/CPI, Sensex and NABARD
  • Universe and geomorphology theory (7 questions combined)
  • Vedic age, Guptas, Sangam and Buddhism/Jainism chapters in depth (3–4 each)
  • Governors-General biographies; newspapers and personalities lists
  • Scientists and inventions (6 of 8 came in 2018 alone)
  • Sports and sociology in Current Affairs

Skip entirely

  • Reading any Ghatna Chakra chapter start to finish without a PYQ reason
  • Restarting Laxmikanth, Spectrum or NCERT sets — use one chapter only for a real concept gap
  • Extra current-affairs sources beyond iDrishti/Drishti
  • Deep macroeconomic theory, balance-of-payments mechanics
  • Maps of regions no PYQ has touched in eight years

When to start Current Affairs

Study-day 26, then a dedicated block

  • Start once Polity, Geography, Environment and Economy are ticked (about study-day 26). These four carry most of the static overlap: new schemes, reports, protected areas, Budget and Survey data.
  • Until study-day 40: 45 minutes a day from iDrishti/Drishti, oldest month first, covering the last 12–15 months up to the exam.
  • Study-days 41–47: the dedicated block in the Current Affairs section above — finish the remaining months, then revise the six CA lists.
  • Reverse-learn from the 199 CA PYQs: they ask ministry, partner country, place, and “first/largest”. Record exactly those details for each item.
  • Integrate as you go: each CA item goes into the matching static sheet too (new scheme → Economy scheme list; new Ramsar site → Environment UP list; defence exercise → CA defence list).

When to start full mocks

Trigger, not a date

Start only when all of these are ticked:

Then, over study-days 48–56

  • Sit the 2025 and 2024 papers as full timed papers first — they test speed on the new all-format pattern.
  • Then 6 full-length mocks on alternate days: take and score on day one; on day two, log every error by cause and fix the matching note sheet.
  • Track accuracy by subject. A subject that stays weak across two mocks gets its P1 sheets revised again, not a new source.
